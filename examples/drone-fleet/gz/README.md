# fleet-gz: the port fleet in one gz world

The quads and rovers fly and drive in one gz sim world of Maasvlakte 2 (pod
`fleet-gz`). Their cameras are cameras in that world: the vehicle's FPV and
the photos the vision model reads come from where it really is.

```text
pod fleet-gz                                              each vehicle's pod
  gz sim (world, vehicles, cameras) ◀── UDP fdm_port_in ── SITL --model=JSON:<fleet-gz>
     └─ <save> PNG ─▶ /frames/<vehicle>/{fpv,nadir}
  fpv-server.py :8081  /fpv/<vehicle> (MJPEG), /latest/<vehicle>.jpg
  ais-bridge.py        ships and containers into the world (PR D)
```

| File | Is |
|---|---|
| `Dockerfile` | gz sim 8.11 (`osrf/ros:jazzy-desktop-full`), ArduPilot's gz plugin (pinned, one patch), ArduCopter and ArduRover 4.7.2 SITL for the benches, pymavlink, Pillow, the world, the models, the scripts |
| `worlds/maasvlakte2.sdf`, `models/maasvlakte2` | the port (generated by `../port3d`) |
| `models/port_quad/model.sdf.tmpl` | the plugin's `iris_with_ardupilot` with three lidars (down, forward, aft: `quad.parm`'s RNGFND1..3) and two cameras: nadir 640×480 at 2 Hz, 84° (the photo, the image's top is the bow), FPV 960×540 at 5 Hz, 10° down |
| `models/port_rover/model.sdf.tmpl` | `port_rover` with an FPV on the bumper and no chase camera |
| `models/port_rover/model.sdf` | the rover bench's model (chase camera, port 9002, lockstep) |
| `fleet-sim` | starts gz, creates each vehicle of `vehicles.json` from its template on its pad, listens to the cameras, logs the real time factor |
| `fpv-server.py` | the cameras' pictures over HTTP, the FPV labelled `FPV: simulator camera · rendered port model` |
| `quad_test.py` | a quad takes off to 10 m, holds, lands |
| `rover-sim`, `oa_test.py`, `plot_run.py` | the rover avoidance bench |
| `test_gz.py` | the scripts' tests without gz |

The templates use `fdm_addr 0.0.0.0` (the SITL is in another pod) and
`lock_step 0` (a stopped SITL does not stop the world). Every SITL runs with
`--home=51.944,3.999,0,0`, the world's origin: the pose in gz puts it on its
pad.

## Benches (wrx80, podman, no cluster)

```bash
podman build -t localhost/fleet-gz:0.1.0 examples/drone-fleet/gz
# The vehicles in the world, the cameras saving:
podman run --rm -v /tmp/gz-frames:/frames localhost/fleet-gz:0.1.0 bash -lc '
  . /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.sh; python3 /gz/fleet-sim --spawn-only --vehicles /gz/vehicles.example.json; sleep 20
  gz topic -l | grep image; ls /frames/*/*'
# The real time factor (gz 8.11 has no `gz stats`):
  gz topic -e -t /world/maasvlakte2/stats | grep real_time_factor
# A quad flies (the app and the chart's files mounted):
podman run --rm -v /tmp/out:/out -v $PWD/examples/drone-fleet/app:/app:ro \
  -v $PWD/examples/helm/drone-fleet/files:/chart:ro localhost/fleet-gz:0.1.0 bash -lc '
  . /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.sh
  python3 -c "import json; json.dump(json.load(open(\"/gz/vehicles.example.json\"))[:1], open(\"/tmp/q.json\", \"w\"))"
  python3 /gz/fleet-sim --spawn-only --vehicles /tmp/q.json; python3 /gz/quad_test.py'
```

Measured 27 Oct 2026 on wrx80, CPU only (llvmpipe), `--cpus 12`, real time factor:

| Scene | Plugin as released | With `nonblocking.patch` |
|---|---|---|
| the world alone | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| one quad, no sensors, no SITL | 0.43 | 0.97 |
| one quad, D9 cameras and lidars | 0.40 | 0.79 |
| six quads on the quays, no sensors | 0.06 | 0.07 |
| six quads falling at 400 m, no sensors | | 0.98 |

The plugin waited for a servo packet on every 1 ms step (1 ms without a
SITL, up to 10 ms with one), per vehicle, in series, even with `lock_step 0`:
the patch does not wait. What is left is the port's collision mesh (74,546
triangles in 442 nodes): every vehicle on it, or inside its bounds, costs
dartsim more than the one before. Bullet's collision detector is cheap but
drives the rover off the quay; ODE flies the quad but is as slow.

`quad_test.py` with one quad: 10.02 m at the top, home 0.01 m from the pad,
no warnings. The rover on its template (`lock_step 0`, the patch, its SITL in
another container as in the pods): 4.06 m/s along `rwg:rwg-yard`.

## The rover that avoids what the map does not know

The routes keep clear of what OpenStreetMap knows. For the rest (a truck, a person, a fallen container) the rover has ArduPilot's own avoidance: its forward rangefinders feed proximity, simple avoidance slows it and stops it short, and BendyRuler steers round. In gz the rangefinders see the port and anything dropped in it.

```text
gz sim (port, rover, box) ── ArduPilotPlugin (JSON, lockstep) ── ArduRover 4.7.2 SITL ── tcp 5760 ── oa_test.py
   gpu_lidar x4 ─▶ rng_1..rng_4 ─▶ RNGFND1..4 (TYPE 100) ─▶ PRX1_TYPE 4 ─▶ AVOID (slow, stop) + OA BendyRuler (steer round)
```

```bash
mkdir -p /tmp/rover-oa
podman run -d --name rover-oa -w /out -v /tmp/rover-oa:/out \
  -v $PWD/examples/drone-fleet/app:/app:ro -v $PWD/examples/helm/drone-fleet/files:/chart:ro \
  localhost/fleet-gz:0.1.0 /gz/rover-sim
podman exec rover-oa bash -c '. /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.sh; python3 /gz/oa_test.py --route rwg:rwg-yard --box 120'
podman exec rover-oa python3 /gz/plot_run.py /out
podman rm -f rover-oa
```

`/tmp/rover-oa` then holds `track.csv`, `events.txt`, `run.json`, `run.png`, SITL's dataflash log (`logs/*.BIN`, with `OABR` for BendyRuler) and the cameras' pictures (`chase/`, `top/`).
